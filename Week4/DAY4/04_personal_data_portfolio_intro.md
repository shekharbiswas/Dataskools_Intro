# 📘 Personal Data Portfolio

Today, you’ll take a step back and reflect on what *you* enjoy most about working with data.  
This is the beginning of building a data portfolio that truly represents who you are — not just what you’ve practiced in class.

Your portfolio is more than a list of tasks. It’s a collection of projects that show your curiosity, your interests, and the way you think.  
When you choose topics that excite you, your work becomes more meaningful — and more memorable to others.

This short reflection will help you discover what type of data projects you might want to pursue next, and what skills you want to keep developing.

## 🧠 What Did You Enjoy Most?

Think about the past few weeks. What parts of the process felt fun, satisfying, or exciting?

- Was it **visualizing** data with charts and colors?
- Was it **exploring** messy spreadsheets to find patterns?
- Or did you enjoy **explaining** insights and writing about what you found?

Write 2–3 sentences reflecting on this in your journal.

<br>


## 🌱 What Kind of Data Interests You?

Different people are drawn to different topics. Take a moment to think about what type of data you'd enjoy working with in the future:

- 📊 Marketing data (ads, sales, social media)?
- 💪 Health and fitness data (steps, food, workouts)?
- 🎓 Education data (learning progress, student feedback)?
- 🌍 Social or environmental data (climate, waste, clean energy)?
- 💡 Or something totally different?

Add a short list to your journal:
- Topics I enjoy:
  - ...
  - ...
  - ...

<br>


## 🎯 Draft a Mini Project Idea

Now try writing a simple mini project idea you’d be excited to explore.

Examples:
- “Analyze my daily screen time and compare it to how I felt each day.”
- “Find which YouTube videos perform best based on title length and views.”
- “Look at student test scores and see if there's a pattern by day of the week.”

Your idea doesn’t need to be perfect — just something you care about. This could become your first **personal portfolio project**.

<br>


## ✅ Add to Your `data_journal.md`

Open or create a file **data_journal.md** in your GitHub folder:

Lemonade-project/

├── analysis_notes/ ← for your written notes and insights
├── README.md ← explains what your project is about
└── journal/ ← for your personal reflection


journal/data_journal.md

Write about:
- What you enjoyed most
- What kind of data you like
- A mini project idea
- Your favorite tools so far (Google Sheets? Canva? SQL?)
- Questions you’re curious to explore next

This journal is just for you — but you can also share parts of it in your README to show your interests and growth.

<br>

## 🧠 Why This Matters

A personal project helps you:
- Learn faster (because you care about the topic)
- Build a unique portfolio
- Stand out when applying to jobs, internships, or bootcamps

And it all starts with a little reflection.

Let your curiosity lead the way!
